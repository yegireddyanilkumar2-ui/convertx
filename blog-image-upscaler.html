What Is Image Upscaling?

Image upscaling is the process of increasing an image's resolution — making it physically larger — while maintaining or improving its visual quality. A 500×500 pixel image upscaled 2× becomes 1000×1000 pixels.

The challenge: when you make an image larger, you're adding pixels that didn't exist in the original. The quality of the result depends entirely on how those new pixels are calculated. Poor upscaling produces blurry, blocky, or distorted output. Good upscaling produces clean, sharp results that look natural.

How ConvertX Upscaling Works

When to Use 2× vs 4× Upscaling

Use 2× when:

  • Your image is slightly too small for its intended use
  • Upscaling a photo for printing (standard print requires 300 DPI)
  • Making a thumbnail larger for social media
  • The original image is already decent quality — 2× preserves it best

Use 4× when:

  • Working with very small images — old screenshots, tiny icons, low-res scans
  • Creating large format prints from a small source
  • Upscaling pixel art or illustrations (works very well)
  • Recovering detail from a heavily compressed image

Output Format Guide

PNG — Best Quality

PNG is a lossless format — every pixel is preserved exactly with no compression artifacts. Choose PNG when quality matters most: logos, screenshots, illustrations, and any image where sharp edges and text need to stay crisp. File sizes will be larger but quality is perfect.

JPG — Smallest File Size

JPG uses lossy compression to dramatically reduce file size. At quality 90+, the difference from the original is invisible to the human eye. Choose JPG for photographs where you need to keep file sizes small — for email, web pages, and social media. Avoid JPG for images with text or sharp geometric shapes.

WEBP — Best of Both

WEBP is Google's modern image format that delivers better quality than JPG at smaller file sizes, and smaller sizes than PNG with near-lossless quality. All modern browsers support WEBP. It's the ideal format for website images — fast loading and great looking.

Tips for Best Upscaling Results

  • Start with the best available original. Upscaling amplifies what's there — a sharp original gives a sharp result, a blurry original gives a blurry result at a larger size.
  • For photos, use JPG or WEBP output at quality 92–95. This keeps file sizes manageable while looking excellent.
  • For graphics with text and sharp edges, use PNG. JPG compression creates artifacts around sharp edges that look bad when upscaled.
  • Don't over-upscale. Going from 100×100 to 400×400 (4×) on a highly compressed photo won't produce a clean result. Upscaling works best when the source image has decent underlying quality.
  • Combine with other tools. After upscaling, use ConvertX's Brightness & Contrast tool to sharpen the image slightly — this can make upscaled images look even crisper.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a file size limit?
No. Since processing happens in your browser using your device's memory, there are no server-imposed limits. Very large images (50MB+) may require a moment to process depending on your device.

Will upscaling make my image sharper?
Upscaling increases resolution — it makes the image larger. It won't add detail that wasn't there. However, high-quality interpolation preserves existing sharpness as you scale up, preventing the blurry or blocky look of simple upscaling methods.

Can I upscale multiple images?
Yes — the tool works on one image at a time. Simply close the tool, reopen it, and upload the next image. Each upscale is instant so batch processing is quick.

Does it work on mobile?
