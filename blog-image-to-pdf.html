Why Convert an Image to PDF?

Images and PDFs serve different purposes. Images are great for display, but PDFs are the universal standard for sharing documents professionally. Here's when you need the conversion:

  • Sending documents by email — a PDF attachment looks professional; a loose JPG doesn't
  • Uploading to portals — many government, legal, and HR systems only accept PDF files
  • Combining multiple images — turn 10 screenshots into one tidy PDF document
  • Preserving layout — PDFs display identically on every device and screen size
  • Digital signatures — signing tools work with PDFs, not raw images
  • Archiving — PDFs are the standard format for long-term document storage

Page Size Options Explained

A4 (210 × 297mm)

The international standard. Use this for any document that will be printed, filed, or shared in Europe, Asia, or Australia. Most office printers and document readers default to A4.

US Letter (8.5 × 11 inches)

The North American standard. Use this if your document will be printed in the US or Canada, or submitted to US-based institutions.

Fit to Image

The PDF page is sized exactly to match your image dimensions. No white borders, no cropping. Best for photos, artwork, or any image where the exact proportions matter.

JPG vs PNG vs WebP — Which Converts Best?

JPG: Best for photos. Smallest file size after conversion. Slight compression artifacts in areas of flat colour, but invisible in real photos.

PNG: Best for screenshots, diagrams, and images with text. Lossless format means every pixel is preserved exactly. Resulting PDF may be larger but perfectly sharp.

WebP: Google's modern format. Excellent quality at small file size. ConvertX handles WebP natively — no need to convert to JPG first.
